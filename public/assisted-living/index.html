Assisted Living at Regency House

Attentive daily care, delivered with dignity, in a licensed residence five minutes from Highway 153.

What It Means Here

For the Moment the House Becomes Too Much

Most families reach us at a similar juncture. A parent remains entirely themselves — still holds opinions on the news and how the coffee ought to be made — yet the house has become too much. Medications are missed. Meals are skipped. A fall becomes the thing everyone quietly fears.

Assisted living is composed for precisely that interval. Your parent keeps a private apartment and their own routine. We attend to the parts that have become unsafe or wearying.

Residences & Rates

Daily Assistance

The Care We Provide

  • Personal care — bathing, dressing, grooming and personal hygiene, at whatever measure is required
  • Medication administration — given on schedule by licensed nurses, never left to chance
  • Transfer and mobility assistance — a steady arm to meals, activities and appointments
  • Incontinence care — attended to discreetly and without fuss
  • Three meals daily — served in our dining room, with refreshments through the day
  • 24-hour pendant call system — in every apartment, answered by staff on the premises
  • Weekly housekeeping and personal laundry
  • Transportation — to medical appointments and community outings

Is It Time?

What Families Often Notice First

If several of these are familiar, assisted living is very likely the right conversation to be having.

  • Medications are being missed, doubled, or quietly set aside
  • There has been a fall — or a near miss, mentioned only later
  • Meals are skipped, or the refrigerator is bare when you visit
  • Bathing has become unsafe, or is being avoided
  • Post and bills accumulate unopened
  • The family caregiver is exhausted, and running out of road

Uncertain? Telephone us and describe the situation. If we are not the right fit, we will say so, and suggest who might be.

How It Proceeds

From First Conversation to Moving In

  • 1. A conversation. You tell us what is happening. We tell you candidly whether assisted living fits, and what it would cost.
  • 2. A private tour. Visit the residence, ideally at mealtime. Meet the care team. Ask whatever you wish.
  • 3. A nursing assessment. We review health history, medications and mobility to compose the care plan and confirm the level of support.
  • 4. Moving in. We assist with the practicalities of the first week — the part families find hardest — and keep you informed thereafter.

In Candour

What We Do Not Provide

Regency House is licensed as an Assisted Care Living Facility. We are not a skilled nursing home, and we do not operate a secure memory care unit. Should your parent require round-the-clock medical care, or a secured setting for advanced dementia, we will tell you so in our first conversation rather than allow you to discover it after a move. Knowing where a community’s limits lie is part of choosing one well.

Common Questions

What Families Ask Us First

What is the difference between assisted living and a nursing home?

Assisted living is for someone who needs help with daily living — bathing, dressing, medications, meals — but does not require round-the-clock skilled nursing or medical treatment. Residents keep their own apartment and as much independence as they safely can. A nursing home provides a considerably higher level of medical care. If your parent’s needs are closer to skilled nursing, we will say so candidly rather than accept the admission.

How much does it cost, and what is included?

The monthly rate is composed from the apartment you choose and the level of daily care required. It includes three meals a day, personal care assistance, medication administration by licensed nurses, weekly housekeeping and laundry, activities, scheduled transportation and a care team present around the clock. We are glad to discuss a specific figure by telephone at (423) 870-0050 — a tour is never a precondition.

May we visit before deciding?

We would be delighted. Private tours are offered daily by appointment, and the most revealing time to visit is around a meal, when the dining room is full and the care team is at work. Do bring your parent if they are able — their impression will tell you more than ours ever could.

Do you accept long-term care insurance, VA benefits or TennCare?

Many families draw on private funds alongside long-term care insurance or VA benefits such as Aid and Attendance, which can meaningfully offset the monthly cost for eligible veterans and surviving spouses. Coverage varies by policy and by programme. For TennCare and the CHOICES programme, please ask what we are currently able to accept, as participation and eligibility rules change.

What if my parent requires more care later?

Care needs change, and we reassess regularly rather than waiting for a crisis. Should the level of assistance increase, we adjust the care plan and discuss with you what that means for the monthly rate before any change takes effect. If a resident should ever need more than an assisted living licence permits us to provide, we help the family plan the next step.

How soon can someone move in?

That depends on current availability and on a brief nursing assessment to confirm assisted living is the right fit. We have welcomed residents within a few days when circumstances were pressing. Telephone (423) 870-0050 and ask what is available now.
