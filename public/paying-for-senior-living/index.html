Senior Living Financing Guide

How to Pay for Assisted Living

The options Hamilton County families use to pay for senior living, explained in plain language, with the current figures, where each one comes from, and where to get free help.

Start Here

Most families combine two or three sources

Very few families pay for assisted living from a single account. Monthly income such as Social Security or a pension usually covers part of the cost, and savings, the family home, insurance or a veterans benefit fill the rest. The right mix depends on your parent’s income, assets, health and military service, and it often changes over time.

Regency House keeps its side simple: one inclusive monthly rate set by the apartment, with no care levels, quoted by phone and confirmed in writing. We are a private-pay community, and we welcome participants enrolled in PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly. How the rate works is explained on our Residences & Rates page; this guide is about where the money comes from.

Most common

Savings, income & retirement accounts

Most assisted living residents in the United States pay privately, from a combination of Social Security, pensions, savings and retirement accounts (National Council on Aging). The first step is simply to add up a parent’s reliable monthly income, then see how far savings would stretch to cover the difference between that income and the monthly rate.

  • Social Security and pensions. Benefits rise with the annual cost-of-living adjustment; the Social Security Administration set the adjustment payable from January 2026 at 2.8% (SSA, October 2025). If a parent can no longer manage money, SSA can appoint a representative payee, often a family member. A power of attorney alone does not give you authority over Social Security funds.
  • IRAs and 401(k)s. Withdrawals from traditional accounts are taxable income. Required minimum distributions generally begin at age 73. A large withdrawal in one year can push a parent into a higher tax bracket, so spreading withdrawals across tax years, with a tax professional’s help, often saves money.
  • Investments and transfers. Selling taxable investments can create capital gains. Keep records of any large gift or transfer: the VA reviews transfers made in the three years before a pension claim, and TennCare reviews five years, so a well-meant gift now can delay a benefit later.

Often the largest source

Selling or renting the family home

Selling turns home equity into a lump sum that can fund years of care. Up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for a married couple filing jointly) can be excluded from tax on a main home that the owner lived in for two of the five years before the sale. There is a rule written for exactly this situation: if the owner became unable to care for themselves and lived in the home for at least 12 months of those five years, time spent in a licensed care facility counts toward the two-year test (IRS Publication 523).

Renting keeps the house in the family and adds monthly income. Rent is taxable, and expenses such as repairs, insurance, property tax and a management company’s fee are deductible (IRS Topic 414). Two cautions: somebody has to manage the property, and renting for several years can reduce the tax exclusion when the home is eventually sold.

Bridge loans & home equity lines

A bridge loan is short-term financing, usually secured by the house, that covers the move and the first months of fees until the home sells. Terms commonly run six to twelve months, with higher interest and closing costs than a regular mortgage (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau). It solves a timing problem, not an affordability problem.

A home equity loan or line of credit (HELOC) borrows against the home and requires monthly payments. The CFPB notes that falling behind can put the home at risk, and that the balance must be paid off when the home is sold. Both tools work best when a sale is already planned.

Reverse mortgages (HECM)

A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage lets homeowners 62 and older turn equity into cash with no monthly mortgage payment while they live in the home. For 2026 the FHA maximum claim amount is $1,249,125 (HUD, December 2025). Counseling with a HUD-approved HECM counselor is required before the loan is made: (800) 569-4287.

The catch for assisted living: the home must stay the borrower’s principal residence. If the borrower lives in assisted living for more than 12 consecutive months and no co-borrower remains in the house, the loan comes due (CFPB). A reverse mortgage therefore suits a couple where one spouse stays home, or a planned move some years away, far better than a single homeowner moving now.

Check the policy

Long-term care insurance

Most long-term care policies begin paying when the insured person needs help with two of six activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, continence, and moving in and out of bed) or has a cognitive impairment, after an elimination period that is commonly 30 to 90 days (Administration for Community Living). Many policies cover assisted living; older ones sometimes cover only nursing homes, so read the definitions in the policy itself.

Premiums can be deducted as medical expenses up to age-based limits. For 2026 the limits are $4,960 for ages 61 to 70 and $6,200 for 71 and older (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Tennessee also takes part in the Long-Term Care Partnership Program, under which a qualifying policy lets the owner protect some assets if TennCare is ever needed. Regency House does not bill insurers; most policies reimburse the policyholder, who then pays the community.

Life insurance & hybrid policies

  • Accelerated death benefits. Many policies let a chronically or terminally ill policyholder draw part of the death benefit early. Whatever is used reduces what heirs receive, and the insurer’s definition of “chronically ill” is what counts (National Association of Insurance Commissioners).
  • Cash value. A permanent (whole or universal) policy can be borrowed against or surrendered for its cash value, less any fees. A loan that is never repaid comes out of the death benefit.
  • Life settlements. The owner sells the policy to a third party for more than its cash value but less than the death benefit. Tennessee regulates life settlement providers and brokers through the Department of Commerce and Insurance (TDCI). Compare several offers, and check the tax and benefit effects first.
  • Hybrid life and long-term care policies pay toward care out of the death benefit and leave the rest to heirs. They usually require a large single premium, so they are a planning tool for families with time, not a quick fix.

Annuities

An immediate annuity turns a lump sum into guaranteed monthly income that can go toward the monthly rate for life or a set period. Deferred annuities usually carry surrender charges, and replacing one can restart them. Be cautious about anyone selling an annuity or a trust as a way to “qualify” for VA or TennCare benefits: the VA warns that these “pension poaching” arrangements can cost a family its benefits and its savings. A fee-only financial adviser or an elder law attorney can review an offer with no stake in the sale.

For veterans & surviving spouses

VA Aid & Attendance pension

Aid and Attendance is a monthly, tax-free addition to the VA pension for wartime veterans and their surviving spouses who need help with daily activities (VA). Eligibility depends on at least one day of wartime service during a recognized period, an honorable discharge, age or disability, and income and net worth within VA limits. It is paid to the veteran or spouse, not to the community, and families use it toward the monthly rate.

Maximum annual pension with Aid & Attendance, effective December 1, 2025 through November 30, 2026 (VA veterans pension rates and survivors pension rates)
Veteran, no dependents$29,093 a year
Veteran with a spouse or dependent$34,488 a year
Surviving spouse, no dependents$18,697 a year
Net worth limit$163,699

VA pays the maximum minus the claimant’s countable income, and unreimbursed medical and care expenses, including assisted living fees, can reduce countable income substantially. VA reviews asset transfers made in the 36 months before a claim. The rates change every December 1 with the Social Security adjustment.

Filing is free. Use a VA-accredited representative or a County Veterans Service Officer (Tennessee lists them through the Department of Veterans Services). VA benefits line: 800-827-1000. Nobody should charge you to prepare or file a pension claim.

Tax deductions, dependents & HSAs

  • Medical expense deduction. Qualified long-term care services for a chronically ill person, provided under a plan of care from a licensed health care practitioner, count as medical expenses. Taxpayers who itemize can deduct medical expenses above 7.5% of adjusted gross income (IRS Publication 502). When a resident needs help with at least two activities of daily living, a large share of the monthly rate may qualify; how much depends on the facts, so ask a tax professional and keep the care plan.
  • Claiming a parent as a dependent. If you provide more than half of a parent’s support and their gross income is under the limit the IRS sets for the year, you may be able to claim them and deduct the medical expenses you pay on their behalf (IRS Publication 501). The parent does not have to live with you.
  • Health savings accounts. HSA money can pay, tax-free, for qualified long-term care services and, within the age limits above, long-term care insurance premiums for you, a spouse or a dependent (IRS Publication 969).

Family contributions & care agreements

Adult children often share the monthly cost, and a short written agreement about who pays what, and what happens if one person’s circumstances change, prevents most of the arguments that come later. If a relative is paid for caring for a parent at home before or alongside a move, a written personal care agreement at a fair hourly rate protects everyone and keeps those payments from being treated as gifts if a benefit application follows (AARP). An elder law attorney can draft one, and the National Academy of Elder Law Attorneys lists members in the Chattanooga area.

What Medicare & TennCare do and don’t cover

Medicare does not pay for custodial care, meaning help with bathing, dressing, meals and medications, or for room and board in assisted living (Medicare.gov). It still covers what it always has for someone living at Regency House: doctor visits, hospital care, therapy, home health visits ordered by a doctor, and prescriptions through Part D, as well as short skilled nursing stays after a qualifying hospital stay.

TennCare CHOICES is Tennessee’s Medicaid program for long-term services and supports. For people who qualify, it can pay for care services at home or in an assisted living community that contracts with a TennCare health plan; it does not pay for room and board. Financial eligibility in 2026 is an income limit of about $2,982 a month and countable assets of $2,000 for a single applicant, with a five-year look-back on transfers, and applicants must also meet the state’s nursing-home level of care (TennCare CHOICES). In Hamilton County, applications start with the Area Agency on Aging and Disability (see free help below). Regency House does not participate in TennCare CHOICES and does not bill Medicare, TennCare or Medicaid. We explain them here because families ask, and because a benefits counselor can tell you whether they matter for your plan.

Accepted at Regency House

PACE at Regency House

PACE stands for the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly. It is a Medicare and Medicaid program that gives older adults who would otherwise qualify for a nursing home a single team of doctors, nurses, therapists, social workers and aides who coordinate all of their care, so they can keep living in the community (Medicare.gov). Participants attend a PACE center for medical care, meals and activities on scheduled days, and the program arranges medications, specialists, transportation to its center and appointments, and hospital care when it is needed. In Hamilton County the PACE organization is Ascension Living Alexian PACE in Chattanooga; the state program page is at TennCare.

To enroll, a person generally must be 55 or older, live in the PACE organization’s service area (for Alexian PACE, that includes Hamilton County), meet Tennessee’s nursing-home level of care as assessed by the state, and be able to live safely in the community with PACE’s support (Medicaid.gov). The PACE team completes the assessment and handles enrollment; Regency House does not decide eligibility. People with Medicaid pay no monthly premium for PACE; people with Medicare only, or neither, pay a monthly amount set by the program, which its enrollment staff explain before anyone signs.

What this means here: Regency House welcomes participants enrolled in PACE. A PACE participant lives in their own apartment at Regency House with our meals, housekeeping and 24-hour care team, keeps their PACE care team for medical care, and the program coordinates with us on medications and transportation to the PACE center. The monthly rate for the apartment and our services is arranged with the family and PACE according to the participant’s enrollment; call (423) 870-0050 and we will walk through how it works for your situation. If your parent is not yet enrolled, Alexian PACE can tell you within a short conversation whether they are likely to qualify.

Questions Families Ask

Paying for senior living: FAQ

How do people pay for assisted living in Tennessee?

Most families pay privately by combining Social Security, pensions, savings, retirement accounts and the proceeds of selling a home. Long-term care insurance and the VA Aid and Attendance pension can add money, and some families use life insurance values or home equity. TennCare CHOICES can pay for services, not room and board, and only at communities that contract with a TennCare plan. PACE participants have their medical care coordinated and paid through the PACE program.

Does Medicare pay for assisted living?

No. Medicare does not cover custodial care or room and board in assisted living. It still covers doctor visits, hospital care, therapy and prescriptions while you live there.

Does Regency House accept PACE?

Yes. Regency House welcomes participants enrolled in PACE, the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly, which in Hamilton County is Ascension Living Alexian PACE. The PACE team decides eligibility: generally age 55 or older, living in the service area, and meeting the state nursing-home level of care. Call (423) 870-0050 and we will explain how a PACE participant’s stay is arranged.

Does Regency House accept Medicaid, TennCare or insurance?

No. Regency House is a private-pay community that also welcomes PACE participants. It does not participate in TennCare CHOICES and does not bill Medicaid, TennCare, Medicare, the VA or insurance companies. Some families receive an insurance reimbursement or a VA benefit themselves and use it toward the monthly rate; whether one applies is between the family and the insurer or the VA, and a benefits counselor can confirm it.

Can VA Aid and Attendance help pay for assisted living?

It can help a family’s budget. Aid and Attendance is paid to the veteran or surviving spouse, not to the care provider. For December 1, 2025 through November 30, 2026, the maximum is $29,093 a year for a single veteran, $34,488 for a veteran with a spouse and $18,697 for a surviving spouse, less countable income. Filing is free through a VA-accredited representative or a County Veterans Service Officer.

Does long-term care insurance cover assisted living?

Many policies do once the insured needs help with two of six daily activities or has a cognitive impairment, after an elimination period. Most reimburse the policyholder rather than the community, so ask the insurer how your policy pays and what documentation it needs from the community.

Can a reverse mortgage pay for assisted living?

Usually not for a single homeowner’s permanent move, because the home must remain the borrower’s principal residence and the loan comes due after 12 consecutive months away. It can help when a spouse stays at home or before a move. Counseling with a HUD-approved counselor is required.

Is assisted living tax deductible?

Sometimes. Qualified long-term care services for a chronically ill person under a practitioner’s plan of care count as medical expenses, deductible above 7.5% of adjusted gross income for people who itemize. Residents who need help with two or more daily activities often qualify for a large share of the monthly rate. Ask a tax professional.

Where can Chattanooga-area families get free help with senior care costs?

Call the Tennessee Commission on Aging and Disability line at 1-866-836-6678 to reach the Southeast Tennessee Area Agency on Aging and Disability, or Tennessee SHIP at 1-877-801-0044 for Medicare questions. Veterans can call VA at 800-827-1000 or work with a free accredited representative. For PACE, contact Ascension Living Alexian PACE in Chattanooga.
