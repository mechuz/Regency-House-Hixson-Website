What It Means Here

For the Moment the House Becomes Too Much

Most families reach us at the same point. A parent is still entirely themselves — still has opinions about the news and how the coffee should be made — but the house has become too much. Medications get missed. Meals get skipped. A fall becomes the thing everyone is quietly afraid of.

Assisted living is built for exactly that gap. Your parent keeps a private apartment and their own routine. We handle the parts that have become unsafe or exhausting.

Daily Support

What We Help With

  • Personal care — bathing, dressing, grooming and personal hygiene, at whatever level is needed
  • Medication administration — given on schedule by licensed nurses, not left on a counter
  • Transfer and mobility assistance — help getting safely to meals, activities and appointments
  • Incontinence care — handled discreetly and without fuss
  • Three meals daily — served in a shared dining room, with snacks available
  • 24-hour pendant call system — in every apartment, answered by staff on site
  • Weekly housekeeping and personal laundry
  • Transportation — to medical appointments and community outings

Is It Time?

Signs Families Tell Us About

If several of these sound familiar, assisted living is usually the right conversation to be having.

  • Medications are being missed, doubled, or quietly stopped
  • There has been a fall — or a near miss nobody mentioned at first
  • Meals are skipped, or the fridge is bare when you visit
  • Bathing has become unsafe, or is being avoided
  • Bills and mail are piling up unopened
  • The family caregiver is exhausted and running out of road

Not sure? Call and describe the situation. If we are not the right fit, we will say so and point you toward what is.

How It Works

From First Call to Move-In

  • 1. A conversation. You tell us what is happening. We tell you honestly whether assisted living fits, and what it would cost.
  • 2. A tour. Come see the community, ideally at mealtime. Meet the care team. Ask the awkward questions.
  • 3. A nurse assessment. We review health history, medications and mobility to build the care plan and confirm the level of support.
  • 4. Move in. We help with the logistics of the first week — the part families find hardest — and keep you updated after.

Being Straight With You

What We Do Not Do

Regency House is licensed as an Assisted Care Living Facility. We are not a skilled nursing home, and we do not operate a secure memory care unit. If your parent needs round-the-clock medical care, or a secured setting for advanced dementia, we will tell you plainly on the first call rather than let you find out after a move. Knowing where a community’s limits are is part of choosing one.

Common Questions

What Families Ask Us First

What is the difference between assisted living and a nursing home?

Assisted living is for someone who needs help with daily living — bathing, dressing, medications, meals — but does not need round-the-clock skilled nursing or medical treatment. Residents keep their own apartment and as much independence as they safely can. A nursing home provides a much higher level of medical care. If your parent’s needs are closer to skilled nursing, we will tell you that honestly rather than take the admission.

How much does it cost, and what is included?

Your monthly rate is based on the apartment you choose and the level of daily care needed. It includes three meals a day, personal care assistance, medication administration by licensed nurses, weekly housekeeping and laundry, activities, scheduled transportation and 24-hour staffing. Call (423) 870-0050 for a specific quote — we will not make you sit through a tour to get a number.

Can we come and see it before deciding?

Please do. Tours run daily by appointment, and the most useful time to come is around a meal, so you can see the dining room full and meet the staff who would be caring for your parent. Bring your parent if they are able — their reaction tells you more than ours ever could.

Do you accept long-term care insurance, VA benefits or TennCare?

Many families use private funds alongside long-term care insurance or VA benefits such as Aid and Attendance, which can meaningfully offset the monthly cost for eligible veterans and surviving spouses. Coverage varies by policy and by programme. For TennCare and the CHOICES programme, call and ask what we can currently accept, as participation and eligibility rules change.

What if my parent needs more care later on?

Care needs change, and we reassess regularly rather than waiting for a crisis. If the level of help needed increases, we adjust the care plan and talk with you about what that means for the monthly rate before anything changes on an invoice. If a resident ever needs more than an assisted living licence allows us to provide, we help the family plan the next step.

How quickly can someone move in?

It depends on current availability and on a short nursing assessment to confirm assisted living is the right fit. We have moved residents in within a few days when a situation was urgent. Call (423) 870-0050 and ask what is open right now.
