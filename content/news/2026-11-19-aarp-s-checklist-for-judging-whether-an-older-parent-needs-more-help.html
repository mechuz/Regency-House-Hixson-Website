AARP's caregiving guide lists the signs relatives can check during a visit when they wonder whether a parent is still managing alone. It covers changes in appearance and hygiene, unexplained weight loss, bruises or burns that may come from struggling with cookware, expired food in the refrigerator, unopened mail and late bills, new dents on the car, missed or unfilled prescriptions, and pulling back from friends and hobbies. The guide suggests comparing notes with siblings, using video calls between visits to notice gradual change, and asking the parent what help they would accept rather than announcing a plan.

Why it matters: Thanksgiving is often the first long visit of the year; a shared checklist turns vague worry into specific things a family can act on together.
