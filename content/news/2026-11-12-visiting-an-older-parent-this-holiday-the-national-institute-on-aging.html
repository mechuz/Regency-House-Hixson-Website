The National Institute on Aging's guide for families describes what a longer visit can reveal that phone calls hide. It suggests looking at whether meals are being cooked safely, whether the home is clean and in repair, whether medications are being taken on schedule, and whether mail and bills are piling up. It also notes that an older person who is struggling with mood or memory can often rally for a short call but not for several days together. The guide encourages families to raise concerns gently, involve the parent in decisions and start with the parent's own doctor when something seems off.

Why it matters: Thanksgiving is the one time many families spend days, not minutes, with a parent; this checklist turns that visit into useful information rather than a worry list.
