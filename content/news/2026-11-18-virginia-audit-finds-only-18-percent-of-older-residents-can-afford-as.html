Virginia's legislative audit office reviewed the state's assisted living sector and reported that only about 18 percent of older Virginians have enough income to afford the average rate in their region, with the share ranging from roughly a third in some northern suburbs to single digits in the rural southwest. The review also recommended tighter state oversight and clearer staffing standards, while the state's provider association cautioned that new mandates would raise operating costs. The report is one of the most detailed looks any state has taken at who can and cannot pay for assisted living.

Why it matters: Affordability and staffing standards vary widely by state and region, so families should ask any community how its rate is set and how it is staffed, not assume a national average applies.
