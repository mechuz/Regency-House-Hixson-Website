The CDC's guidance for the 2026-27 season keeps its preference that people 65 and older receive one of three enhanced flu vaccines rather than a standard-dose shot: the high-dose vaccine, the adjuvanted vaccine or the recombinant vaccine. The agency says studies suggest these work better in older adults, whose immune response to the standard shot is weaker, and that if none of the three is on hand, any age-appropriate flu vaccine is better than skipping it. Older adults account for most flu hospitalizations and deaths each year, and the CDC says vaccination by the end of October is ideal but still worthwhile later in the season.

Why it matters: If a parent has not had a flu shot yet, ask the pharmacy or doctor specifically for the senior version; it is a quick errand that lowers the chance of a hospital stay this winter.
