Senior Housing News reports on a National Investment Center panel about housing for older adults who earn too much for subsidized programs but struggle with market-rate senior living. Panelists said the industry does not lack workable ideas. Communities that serve this group keep costs down by offering a simpler menu of services and fewer customization options. What holds back growth is financing and the ability to operate at scale, since lenders and investors still favor higher-end projects. The piece reflects a wider trend in the trade press this fall: new construction is leaning upscale while mid-priced supply grows slowly.

Why it matters: Families shopping for assisted living in 2026 are choosing from a market with little new mid-priced supply, so starting the search early and asking what services are included matters more than ever.
