NPR discussed a new AARP survey of more than 2,500 family caregivers that puts their average out-of-pocket spending at about $11,500 a year, roughly 60 percent higher than five years ago. Food, housing and medical costs make up the largest share, and about half of caregivers say they have cut savings, worked extra hours or drawn on retirement money to cover it. Caregivers of someone with dementia spend more. The segment also walks through what paid help costs nationally, from hourly home care to adult day programs and residential care, and why families often reach a point where caring at home no longer adds up.

Why it matters: If caregiving costs are climbing at home, it is worth comparing the full monthly picture against a residential option before a crisis forces the decision.
