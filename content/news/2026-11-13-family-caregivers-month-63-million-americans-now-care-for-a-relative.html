November is National Family Caregivers Month, and the most complete national picture comes from the AARP and National Alliance for Caregiving study of 6,858 caregivers. It counts 63 million Americans, nearly one adult in four, providing ongoing care for an adult or child with a serious condition or disability, 20 million more than ten years earlier. The typical caregiver is 51, gives about 27 hours a week, and most do it while holding a paying job. More than half perform medical or nursing tasks at home, yet only about one in five received any training, and nearly half report a financial hit from caregiving.

Why it matters: If caring for a parent has quietly become a second job, you are in the majority; a respite stay or an assisted living move is often what lets a family keep caring without burning out.
