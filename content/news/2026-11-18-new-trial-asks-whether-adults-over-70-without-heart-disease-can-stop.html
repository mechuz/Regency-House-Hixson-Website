A European trial presented at the American Heart Association's annual meeting followed about 1,900 adults aged 70 and older who took a statin only to prevent a first heart attack or stroke and who had at least two other chronic conditions. Over roughly two years, those who stopped the drug met the study's safety threshold, though a small increase in cardiovascular events was observed and the authors said the findings do not support a blanket recommendation to stop. People with existing heart disease were not included. Researchers framed the result as a reason for an individual conversation between an older patient and their clinician about which medications still serve a purpose.

Why it matters: Medication reviews matter for older adults; this is a question to raise with a doctor, not a change to make on your own.
